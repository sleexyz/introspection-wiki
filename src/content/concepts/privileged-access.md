Privileged access is a stricter requirement than a causal link. On this view a self-report counts as introspection only if the model has access to itself that others lack, so that it does better at describing itself than an outside predictor with the same information could.

This page currently reflects only how [Atkinson et al. (2026)](/papers/atkinson2026-identifying-introspection) describe the debate. It will be filled in from the papers themselves.

- [Binder et al. (2024)](/papers/binder2024-looking-inward) report that models trained to predict their own behavior outperform other models trained to predict it.
- [Song, Hu & Mahowald (2025)](/papers/song2025-fail-to-introspect) tested metalinguistic judgments and found the same-model advantage was largely explained by how similar the models were.
- [Song et al. (2025)](/papers/song2025-privileged-self-access) argue that introspection should be defined by privileged self-access, not merely by a causal link, and apply that critique to the temperature example of [Comsa & Shanahan (2025)](/papers/comsa2025-speak-of-introspection).

A paper can test [grounding](/concepts/grounding) without testing privileged access, and the evidence cards on this wiki record the two separately.
